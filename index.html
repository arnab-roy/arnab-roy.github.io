Research

Research interests and contributions

My research spans cryptographic foundations, protocol security, and the engineering of high-assurance systems. A recurring goal is to make strong security guarantees practical and deployable.

Recent work

  • Batch threshold encryption. Developing efficient constructions based on partial-fraction techniques, with applications to decentralized protocols.
  • Post-quantum migration. Designing backward-compatible paths for EdDSA-based chains to adopt quantum-safe authorization.
  • Verified confidential systems. Optimizing confidential-transfer protocols and proving critical components in Lean.

Cryptography and high-assurance systems

Zero-knowledge proofs, threshold encryption, digital signatures, privacy, authentication, and post-quantum cryptography. Current work includes protocol design, cryptographic formalization, auditing, and production deployment.

Formal methods for security protocols

Computational protocol security, Protocol Composition Logic, inductive proofs, automated decision procedures, and machine-checked proofs. My Stanford Ph.D. developed formal methods for proving the computational security of network protocols.

AI, optimization, and specialized computing

Clustering, outlier detection, binary matrix factorization, local-search heuristics, and higher-order binary optimization on specialized hardware.
